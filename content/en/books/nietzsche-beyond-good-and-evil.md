*Review of a public-domain book. Written by Captain PhD, 10 November 2026.*

## In brief
*Beyond Good and Evil* appeared in 1886 and is built from short aphorisms rather than one long argument. Nietzsche claims that philosophers often dress personal prejudices and moral wishes in the form of neutral reason. He wants us to ask why we believe what we believe, and which needs lie behind it.

## About the book
The book has nine parts, from criticism of philosophers' prejudices to thoughts on morality, religion, science and "free spirits". Nietzsche questions the idea of objective truth without a standpoint and holds that all knowledge is seen from a perspective. He also discusses the "will to power" and how moral systems can express particular types of people. The style is provocative and easily misunderstood; many of his ideas have been misused by others. The Project Gutenberg edition is Helen Zimmern's early twentieth-century translation.

## From the bookshelf to daily life
This is our own reading, not Nietzsche's.

**Programming.**
- Nietzsche's question "why do you believe this?" suits technical choices. Many choices of language, framework and architecture rest on habit, fashion and a wish for recognition, not on neutral analysis.
- The idea of perspective reminds us that measurements and dashboards always show something from a particular standpoint, and hide something else.

**Seafaring.**
- Rule 7(c) says assumptions shall not be made on the basis of scanty information. It is a practical antidote to "prejudices" about a situation, for example that an approaching ship will surely do what I expect.
- Rule 5 requires a look-out by all available means. Assuming you see the whole picture from one standpoint is dangerous. Several sources give a truer picture.

## Objections
- Nietzsche is often unclear and self-contradictory, and the aphorisms leave little room for systematic argument.
- His views on women, the masses and the "weak" are condescending and hard to read without objecting.
- If everything is perspective, what is the basis of his own claims?
