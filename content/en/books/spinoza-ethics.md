*Review of a public-domain book. Written by Captain PhD, 10 November 2026.*

## In brief
*Ethics* was published after Spinoza's death in 1677. It is built like a geometry textbook, with definitions, axioms and proofs. Spinoza argues that everything that exists is one and the same, which he calls God or Nature, and that human freedom lies in understanding what governs us, especially our emotions.

## About the book
The book has five parts: on God, on the mind, on the emotions, on human bondage to the emotions, and on the power of the intellect and freedom. Spinoza holds that we do not have free will in the ordinary sense, because everything has causes. But we can become freer by understanding the causes of our emotions, so that they have less power over us. Elwes's nineteenth-century English translation is open on Project Gutenberg. The text is demanding, and most readers go slowly or alongside an introduction.

## From the bookshelf to daily life
This is our own reading, not Spinoza's.

**Programming.**
- Spinoza's form, with definitions first and proofs after, resembles how we build programs from small, precise parts. Every claim should rest on what has already been established.
- Debugging is understanding causes. When we find why a bug occurs, it loses its power over us. This resembles his thought that understanding makes us freer.

**Seafaring.**
- A sailor learns that the weather cannot be persuaded, only understood. Good decisions come from knowing the forces one is subject to, not from wishing them away.
- Fear and haste are emotions that can steer an officer of the watch. COLREG Rule 7(c) warns against drawing conclusions from scanty information. It is a practical form of letting understanding outweigh first impressions.

## Objections
- The doctrine that everything follows by necessity is hard for many readers to reconcile with responsibility and guilt.
- The proofs in geometrical form often seem artificial, and it is not always clear that they hold.
- The identity of God with nature was seen by many contemporaries as atheism and is still disputed.
