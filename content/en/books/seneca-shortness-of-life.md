## In brief
*De brevitate vitae* is a short text by the Roman Stoic Seneca, written as a letter to a man named Paulinus. The main point is that life is not short, but that we make it short by wasting time. Whoever spends his days on things that do not matter to him reaches the end of life without having lived.

## About the book
Seneca opens with people complaining that nature gave us too little time. He answers that we receive enough if we use it well. He then goes through what people spend time on: greed, ambition to win other people's favour, drink, sloth and a life of constant busyness. The busy, according to Seneca, are those with least time to live, because they are always postponing. He also points out that we guard money and property but give time away to anyone. The Project Gutenberg edition collects several of his "minor dialogues" in Aubrey Stewart's translation; *On the Shortness of Life* is one of them.

## From the bookshelf to daily life
This is our own reading, not Seneca's.

**Programming.**
- Being busy is not the same as making progress. A project can have many open tasks, meetings and tools without anything important getting finished. Seneca would ask: which of this do you actually want built?
- Putting things off "until later" is familiar to anyone who has written code: the tests, the documentation, the clean-up. Technical debt is a kind of deferred time.

**Seafaring.**
- Rule 5 says a proper look-out shall be kept at all times. Attention on watch is a scarce resource that can be wasted on a phone, paperwork and small talk. Seneca would call that giving away your time without noticing.
- A voyage brings long stretches of routine. Using free time on board deliberately, to read, train or learn, is a practical way of following Seneca's advice.

## Objections
- Seneca was rich and powerful in Rome, among other things as tutor and adviser to the emperor Nero. Many have asked whether he lived as he wrote.
- The advice can sound easy to someone with time and money. Many people do not get to choose how they spend their time.
- The text condemns busyness harshly. Sometimes work and care for others are the very meaning of life.
