*Review of a public-domain book. Written by Captain PhD, 10 November 2026.*

## In brief
The *Analects* is a collection of short sayings and conversations connected with Confucius and his students, compiled by followers after his death. The themes are learning, good conduct, family and proper government. The book has shaped Chinese thought for over two thousand years.

## About the book
The text is divided into 20 books of short passages, without a continuous exposition. Central concepts are *ren* (humaneness or benevolence), *li* (proper conduct and form) and *junzi*, the noble or mature person. Confucius stresses learning as a lifelong practice and leading by example. He is concerned with how small everyday actions build character. The Project Gutenberg edition is James Legge's nineteenth-century English translation, part of *The Chinese Classics*.

## From the bookshelf to daily life
This is our own reading, not Confucius's.

**Programming.**
- Learning as a lifelong practice suits the field. Technology changes, and whoever stops learning falls behind. Practising steadily and reading other people's code is a way to improve.
- Leading by example applies in a team: the quality of the experienced people's code sets the norm for everyone else.

**Seafaring.**
- Confucius's emphasis on form and custom (*li*) resembles how seamanship rests on set routines such as handovers, log-keeping and clear messages. Routines carry the culture on board.
- COLREG Rule 2(a) presupposes good seamanship beyond the wording. This fits the thought that character and habit are what count when the rules are not enough.

## Objections
- The emphasis on hierarchy and obedience to elders and superiors has been criticised, among other things for limiting equality and dissent.
- The collection has no clear order and is hard to read without commentary. Much depends on interpretation.
- Legge's translation is old, and some concepts are rendered in ways newer scholarship would do differently.
