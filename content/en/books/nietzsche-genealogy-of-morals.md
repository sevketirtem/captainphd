*Review of a public-domain book. Written by Captain PhD, 10 November 2026.*

## In brief
*On the Genealogy of Morals* appeared in 1887 and consists of three essays. Nietzsche asks not first what is right, but how our moral concepts arose historically, and which interests they have served. He wants to show that morality has a history and does not fall from the sky.

## About the book
In the first essay he distinguishes "master morality" from "slave morality" and claims that the concepts of good and evil grew out of the experiences of different groups. In the second he discusses guilt, debt and conscience. In the third he asks what ascetic ideals, such as self-denial, mean and why people choose them. The Project Gutenberg edition is an older English translation by Horace B. Samuel, published in Oscar Levy's collected works. Nietzsche is polemical, and his claims are disputed as history.

## From the bookshelf to daily life
This is our own reading, not Nietzsche's.

**Programming.**
- A genealogy of code resembles reading old code and asking why it became what it is. Many "rules" in a project have a history nobody remembers, and they may have arisen for quite different reasons than the ones given now.
- The question "who does this rule serve?" also fits guidelines in teams and organisations.

**Seafaring.**
- Many routines on board have a history. Some came after accidents, some after insurers' requirements. Knowing the background makes it easier to see what is necessary and what is merely habit.
- The collision regulations also came about through history: they build on experience of collisions. Rule 2(a) shows that ordinary seamanship comes in addition to the wording itself.

## Objections
- Nietzsche's historical claims are speculative and do not hold up as historical scholarship.
- His contemptuous treatment of compassion and of "the weak" is problematic and has been used for bad ends.
- Even if a morality has an unflattering origin, that does not by itself show it is wrong.
